This page is the beginning of a small writing space alongside my materials-science portfolio. Its purpose is simple: to retain ideas that are useful enough to revisit, and to share selected thoughts that may be useful to other people.

Why write?

Technical work often involves many small observations: a question raised by a measurement, an approach that worked, an unexpected result, an explanation that became clearer after discussion, or an idea found during reading. Without a place to record them, many of these useful details disappear quickly.

Writing is also a way to make thinking more precise. It encourages a distinction between what is known, what is assumed, what needs checking, and what remains uncertain. This is valuable both in research and in applied technical work.

What I plan to include

Future posts may include notes on electron microscopy, thin films, scientific data analysis, materials characterisation, conference reflections, professional development, and the practical process of learning. Some entries may be short; others may develop into more detailed technical explanations.

A work in progress

This is not intended to be a finished archive from the first day. It will grow gradually through projects, events, questions, and useful experiences. The aim is to keep the writing clear, honest, and connected to real work.
